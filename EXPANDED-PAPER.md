# Expanded paper trading and corrected research

The existing dashboard at http://127.0.0.1:8791 now has Core portfolio, Markets & hourly paper, Corrected backtests, and Strategy & research tabs. The desktop Trading Dashboard shortcut launches it. No broker or real-money execution is connected.

## Running and collecting

Keep the local server running, computer awake with internet, and the dashboard page open. Checks are requested every minute and market fetches throttled to five minutes; browser suspension can interrupt them. This is not an unattended background trading service. Pause applies to the core, references and experiments and survives restart.

The existing core book and previous observations are preserved. New observations store observation time, price-bar time, equity, cash, positions and strategy version. Trades store the signal bar, fill bar, quantity, reference price and modeled cost. These persist in SQLite under runtime. Historical startup bars warm indicators; they are NEVER replayed as forward fills. The actual forward record starts when each account first observes data. Time must elapse to assess performance; today's installation is not a validated track record.

Use Markets & hourly paper â†’ Recorded observations and fills to inspect an account. The view shows the latest 50 records; the database and read-only /api/record?account=... endpoint retain the complete record. Each recorded observation carries the bar label, the assumed bar completion time, the actual observation time, the cycle outcome, the hours since the previous observed bar and observation, and a heuristic flag for possibly unobserved bars in between. Each fill carries the observation time of its fill bar and of its signal bar. A gap between observations means no marks, signals or fills exist for the bars in between; because held shares are fixed, the equity change across the gap is the net price move of those shares, not a record of the intermediate path. Gap flags for daily accounts count business days and do not reconcile exchange holidays, so a holiday is flagged as a possible gap; hourly gap flags use the account's declared calendar (24/7 or US regular session) from the market intake metadata, which the record endpoint passes automatically. The record's bar completion time for each observation and fill is the execution-time bound actually used for that cycle, persisted with the cycle; it is a lower bound on the actual bar end, not the actual close (13:00 New York for daily bars). A flagged interval may contain unobserved bars, but the flag does not prove bars existed. Each experiment has independent $1,000 virtual capital and a matching buy-and-hold reference. Core benchmarks each start separately with $10,000. Do not sum experiment balances or mistake them for an allocation recommendation. Compare only matched observation dates.

## Coverage and strategies

- Existing core: Strategy C monthly planning across the 65-stock pool, with GLD/TLT defensive rules.
- Hourly intake: 62 initial instruments across individual stocks, US sectors, international equity funds, bonds, commodity funds, real estate, currency funds and 10 crypto references. Instrument metadata is explicit in market_lab.py.
- Hourly trend candidate: 20/80-hour moving-average rule, long or cash.
- Additional crypto candidate: the older SMA200, RSI and MACD scoring rules, entry score >=4 and exit <=0.
- Additional currency-fund candidate: the older Bollinger-band/RSI scoring rules, entry >=4 and exit <=-4. These are US-listed fund proxies, not direct forex execution.
- Both older-score candidates also reset to cash after 24 observed signal bars. This is a signal-duration rule, not a guaranteed maximum real fill-to-fill duration; market closures, missing data and next-bar execution can extend actual exposure.
- Add paper market accepts US-listed stock/fund symbols or USD crypto references. Added instruments get new independent accounts and must pass data checks. Their histories are not in the original comparison until validation is rerun with that intake.

Direct futures, options, direct forex, shorting, leverage, other exchange calendars and unvalidated illiquid instruments are unsupported for execution. Commodity funds incorporate their own fund/roll economics in their prices; they are not simulated futures contracts. Crypto prices use public Coinbase Exchange USD candles, not executable bid/ask quotes. Stock and fund prices use Yahoo adjusted bars. The initial Yahoo crypto feed had missing hours and was replaced before any crypto forward observations; historical crypto comparisons were rerun on Coinbase candles. Hourly bar trading is not high-frequency trading.

## Execution and data assumptions

Targets are long/cash. Fixed fractional adjusted-price units drift between fills. The shared execution function solves post-cost equity so all positions and costs are cash funded. Entry and exit costs are based on actual changed notional. Core/stock/fund base cost is 6 bps per side; crypto base is 30 bps. Hourly comparisons include 3x cost stress. These assumptions are not verified exchange fee/spread quotes.

Signals are generated from completed bars and fill at a later observed close. For every account (core daily, core benchmarks and hourly experiments) the fill bar must also have completed after the signal was actually observed, not merely carry a later label. A provider that publishes a bar late cannot fill a signal that was observed after that bar's close; the cycle outcome then reads "fill held: this bar closed before the queued signal was observed" and the queued target waits for the next completed bar or expires unfilled. No exchange calendar is bundled and daily provider bars carry no close time, so the daily close is taken fail-closed as 13:00 New York, the earliest regular NYSE close. A signal observed after 13:00 New York therefore cannot fill at that day's close and waits for the next completed session: on an early-close day this prevents an impossible fill, and on an ordinary day it delays a fill that a 16:00 assumption would have allowed. A snapshot's explicit bar completion time is untrusted input: it may only be earlier than the derived time, and a later or malformed value holds the whole cycle. A queued signal whose observation time is not recorded (data written before observation times existed) cannot fill; it is held until it expires, then re-planned at an observed bar with provenance. No same-bar execution, retrospective fill recovery, repeated-bar fills, or optimistic stop-touch fills. Queued signals expire after two hours for crypto and 96 hours for exchange-traded hourly instruments; core expiry remains seven days. Expiration requeues at a newly observed bar.

Yahoo and Coinbase hourly bars are start-labelled; the close time is derived explicitly, including the normal US final half-hour bar. A five-minute completion buffer applies. Bar availability and execution timing are separate: a listed bar becomes usable only after its labelled end plus buffer, while the fill guard uses a lower bound on when the bar actually ended. The 12:30 New York start bar is the one ambiguous case (it ends at 13:00 on an early-close day but is labelled 13:30), so its execution bound is 13:00; all other listed bars and all crypto candles use their labels. Stale hourly prices (>100 minutes) cannot generate new fills. Closed stock markets are displayed as closed/stale, while crypto can continue. At least 200 valid completed hourly bars are required. Detected crypto gaps and within-session equity gaps block readiness. Full exchange holiday/early-close calendar reconciliation and intraday execution microstructure are not implemented; provider calendars can cause delayed eligibility. Missing held prices invalidate historical simulations; paper execution is held without inventing a price.

Adjusted prices approximate total-return units, not a broker share/dividend ledger. Taxes, borrow, funding, auction liquidity, market impact and venue-specific settlement are not modeled. The forward source history is retained as first observed rather than rewritten by subsequent provider revisions; corporate-action reconciliation remains necessary before real execution.

## Corrected historical results

Run `python validation_report.py --out runtime/validation` to refresh public source data and reports. Use `--cached` to reproduce calculations on the saved data. Source CSVs and source-code SHA256 hashes are recorded with the report. No parameter optimization or automatic winner promotion is performed.

The corrected allocator and risk-overlay accounting use fixed shares, later-close execution, actual turnover costs and cash-funded positions. The primary report matches current first-observed-session monthly planning. A corrected month-end variant isolates the alternative legacy timing. Benchmarks use identical dates, starting cash, accounting and modeled entry costs: SPY, QQQ, 60/40 SPY/AGG, a static same-pool basket, BIL Treasury bills and zero-interest cash. Positions are marked at the end without a forced liquidation. Sharpe above Treasury bills is shown, alongside other metrics in the saved JSON.

The stock universe is still survivor biased. The same-pool basket does not eliminate that bias. There is no claim of a repaired point-in-time/delisting dataset. Recent period comparisons are retrospective, not untouched out-of-sample evidence. Older research archive results and images are retained as historical artifacts and are not automatically regenerated or validated by this change.

The 60-day requested hourly sample includes 200 warm-up bars, so effective evaluation windows vary by instrument calendar. Compare each strategy only with its own matched reference; do not rank asset performance across unequal windows. All candidates and both cost scenarios are reported. A fresh forward record is the next genuinely unobserved test; more history is required to judge any hourly edge.

## Verification

`python -B -m unittest test_unified test_accounting test_observation_guard` covers the actual-observation-time fill guard on daily, benchmark and hourly accounts (delayed provider bars on ordinary and early-close days, restart persistence, fail-closed legacy rows and their expiry recovery, untrusted bar completion times), record provenance, gap flags and the record endpoint's calendar handling, fixed-share drift, next-bar timing, entry and exit costs, cash constraints, missing held prices, paper/history accounting parity, version separation, pending signal changes, hourly gaps/completion, future-data leakage, forward-only initialization, pause, restart deduplication and existing local HTTP security checks. Tests use synthetic prices and disposable accounts.

The testing-strategy skill was applied to financial invariants, data pipeline failures and HTTP integration. Live execution, order books, full market calendars and long-horizon statistical performance remain outside these tests.
